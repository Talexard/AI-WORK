# Pitch Script: Custom Craft Carpentry & Remodeling

**Business:** Custom Craft Carpentry & Remodeling. Solo operator, residential remodeling and construction.
**Phone:** (937) 561-2262 · **Listed address:** 219 E Court St, Urbana, OH 43078
**The hook:**
- customcraftremodel.com shows Squarespace's **"Website Expired"** page (checked Nov 6, 2026).
- The domain record shows an **expiration date of Dec 26, 2026**.

**Demo link:** `[your Netlify link, e.g. customcraftremodel-preview.netlify.app]`
**Offer:** a one-time price. They own everything, with no monthly fee to you.

Replace `[Your name]` and `[link]` before sending. Send each message yourself, one at a time, not through a mass-texting tool.

---

## 0. Before you reach out (5 minutes)
- [ ] Open customcraftremodel.com on your phone and confirm it still shows "Website Expired." Take a screenshot.
- [ ] Open your demo link on your phone and make sure it loads.
- [ ] Search Google Maps for "Custom Craft Carpentry Urbana." Note their star rating, number of reviews and the owner's first name if it's shown. **If you find the owner's name, use it everywhere below.**
- [ ] **Timing:** contractors are on job sites during the day. The best windows are **7:00–8:00 am** or **5:00–7:00 pm**, Tuesday to Thursday.

## 1. First text (send this first)

> Hi, this is [Your name]. I build websites for local businesses here in the area. I tried to look you up and noticed customcraftremodel.com is showing a "Website Expired" page, so anyone searching for you right now hits a dead end.
>
> I put together a free sample of what a new site could look like for you: [link]
>
> No cost to look and no obligation. If it's useful, I'm happy to talk. Either way, have a good one.

**Why it works:** it's helpful first (you're pointing out a real problem), it's specific (their exact site), and there's no pressure.

---

## 2. Phone call script

Call 1–2 days after the text, or call first if you prefer. Keep it under 5 minutes unless they want to keep talking.

**Opener (be quick and friendly):**
> "Hi, is this the owner of Custom Craft Carpentry? This is [Your name]. I'm a local web designer. I sent you a text the other day about your website. Do you have two minutes, or did I catch you on a job?"

- **If they're busy:** "No problem. When's a better time, tonight or tomorrow morning?" Write it down and call back exactly then.
- **If yes,** continue.

**The problem:**
> "I was looking you up and your website, customcraftremodel.com, is showing a 'Website Expired' message from Squarespace. Did you know about that?"

*Stop and let them answer.* Most owners don't know, or they let it go because it felt like a hassle.

**The deadline (keep it factual, don't use it to scare):**
> "The domain itself, the web address, shows an expiration date of December 26th. It's worth making sure that's set to renew, because if a domain lapses someone else can buy it."

**The demo:**
> "I went ahead and built a sample of what a new site could look like. Did you get a chance to open the link? It works on your phone."

If they haven't opened it: "Open it while we're on the phone. It only takes a second."

Point out three things while they look:
1. "The call button is right at the top, so people can reach you in one tap."
2. "There's a spot for your best project photos. That's what sells remodeling work."
3. "It's built around the fact that *you* do the work. Customers deal with the craftsman, not a crew."

**Ask before you pitch:**
> "Where do most of your jobs come from right now?"
> "When someone hears about you from a neighbor, what do you think they do before they call?" *(Answer: they Google you.)*
> "About what's a typical job worth to you?"

**The offer:**
> "Here's how I do it. It's a one-time price: **$[price]**, no monthly fees to me. You own the site, the domain, everything, all in your name. You send me 6 to 8 photos of your best jobs, I confirm your services and the towns you cover, and it's live within a week."
>
> "If it brings in even one kitchen or bath job, it's paid for itself several times over."

**Close:**
> "Want me to go ahead and get it set up?"

- **If yes:** "Great. I'll text you a short agreement and the invoice for half up front. The rest is due when it goes live. I'll also send a short list of what I need from you."
- **If "let me think about it":** "Of course. What's the main thing you'd want to think through? Is it the price, the timing, or something else?" Answer that one thing, then: "I'll check back Thursday. Sound good?"

---

## 3. Pricing to quote
- **Standard price:** $1,200 one-time for the full site and handoff.
- **Founding-client price (use this for your first 2–3 clients):** **$900**, in exchange for permission to show their site in your portfolio and a short written testimonial once it's live.
- **Payment:** 50% to start and 50% at launch. Card through a Stripe link, or a check.
- **Their only ongoing cost:** the domain, about $15–20 a year, paid directly to the registrar.
- **Optional:** edits after launch at $50 per small change, or $75 an hour. No subscription.

Don't go below $750. Cheap pricing makes it look like low-quality work.

**Day 7:**

**If they reply "not interested" or "stop":**
> No problem at all, thanks for letting me know. Take care!

Then stop. Never text again after a no.

## 6. Objections: what to say

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "I get all my work by word of mouth." | "That's great, and it means people trust you. But those referrals usually look you up before they call. Right now they land on an expired page. This makes sure the referral turns into a phone call." |
| "Too expensive." | "I understand. It's a one-time cost, with no monthly bills to me. If it brings in one job, it's paid for. I can also split it into two payments." |
| "I don't have time." | "That's exactly why I do it all. I need about 20 minutes of your time in total: send photos and answer a few questions. I handle the rest." |
| "My nephew / a friend does websites." | "No problem at all. The sample is yours to look at, and it might give them some ideas. If anything changes, you have my number." *(Then leave it there.)* |
| "I'm too busy for more work right now." | "Good problem to have! A site also lets you pick better jobs, and it's ready for when things slow down. Want me to check back in a month?" |
| "How do I know you're legit?" | "Fair question. I'm local, the sample's already built so you can see my work before paying anything, and you only pay the second half once the site is live and you're happy with it." |
| "Can you just fix the Squarespace one?" | "I can look at it. But renewing Squarespace usually runs a couple hundred dollars a year or more. Mine is one-time, and you'd only pay about $15–20 a year for the domain." |

---

## 7. After they say yes: what to send the same day
1. **A short agreement** (ask Claude for a template): price, what's included, timeline, 50/50 payment, and that ownership transfers to them on final payment.
2. **An invoice or payment link** for 50%.
3. **The intake questions:**
   - 6–8 photos of your best jobs. Before-and-after pairs are best.
   - Which services do you want to show? (Kitchens, baths, trim, decks, additions, repairs: anything to add or remove?)
   - Which towns do you serve?
   - Do you give free estimates? Is texting okay for customers to reach you?
   - Your Google Business Profile, so we can show your real reviews.
   - **Your Squarespace login access**, so we can renew the domain, or you renew it yourself while I walk you through it on the phone.
   - **Does your email use @customcraftremodel.com?** We need to know so we don't break it.

---

## 8. Rules to keep this honest
- Only say things that are true. The expired page and the Dec 26 date are both on record. Don't exaggerate them.
- Never write or fake reviews. Only use real ones, word for word.
- Don't show up at 219 E Court St uninvited unless it's clearly a shop or office. For a solo operator it may be a home.
- Send texts by hand, one at a time, and stop the moment someone asks you to.
