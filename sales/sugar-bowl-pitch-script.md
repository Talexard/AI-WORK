# Pitch Script: Sugar Bowl Ice Cream Parlor & Restaurant

**Phone:** (480) 946-0051
**Email:** sugarbowlscottsdale@yahoo.com
**Address:** 4005 N Scottsdale Rd, Scottsdale, AZ 85251 (a public restaurant, so visiting in person is fine)
**Who to ask for:** "the owner." Today it's run by Carroll Huntress's daughter and son-in-law; their names aren't public. Ask whoever answers for the owner's name and write it down.
**Demo link:** `[your Netlify link, e.g. sugarbowl-preview.netlify.app]`

## Best time to reach them
Restaurants are slammed at lunch and in the evening. Reach out in the slow window:
- **Call or visit Tuesday–Thursday, about 2:30–4:30 pm.** They open at 11 am every day.
- **Avoid** 11:30 am–1:30 pm, 6–9 pm, and all weekend.

## How this pitch differs
The Sugar Bowl is a 68-year-old institution, not a struggling business. **Don't say their site is "bad."** Say it doesn't do the parlor justice, and point to things they'll care about:
1. **The menu is 4 PDF files.** They're hard to read on a phone, and Google can't read them.
2. **There's no tap-to-call** on the phone version.
3. **It's slow** (about 11 seconds in our check).
4. **There's no clear way to book a Fiesta birthday party.**

---

## Option A: Visit in person (strongest)
Restaurants respond best to a real person who is also a customer.

1. Go in during the slow window (Tue–Thu, 2:30–4:30). **Buy something.** You're a customer first.
2. Ask: *"Is the owner around? I'm a local web designer and I made something for the Sugar Bowl."*
3. Show the demo **on your phone.** Hand it to them and let them scroll.
4. Say:
   > "I love this place, so I built a sample of what your website could look like. The big thing is the menu: right now it's four PDFs that are hard to read on a phone. Here it's all on one page, and Google can actually read it. There's also a one-tap call button, directions, an 'open now' badge, and a section to book Fiesta parties."
5. Then: *"Can I text you the link so you can show your family?"* Get their name and cell number.
6. Leave a simple card or note with your name, number and the demo link.

---

## Option B: Phone call

**Opener (to whoever answers):**
> "Hi! Quick question: is the owner available? My name is [Your name]. I'm a local web designer and I put together something for the Sugar Bowl. It'll take two minutes."

- **If the owner's not in:** "No problem. What's the best time to catch them, and can I get their first name?" Then: "Could I get an email or cell number to send them a link?"
- **If they say they're busy:** "Totally understand. I'll call back around 3. Thanks!"

**With the owner:**
> "Hi [Name], thanks for taking my call. I'm [Your name], a web designer here in the Valley. I'm a fan of the Sugar Bowl. I mean, 68 years in the same pink building is incredible."
>
> "I was looking at your website on my phone and noticed the menu is four PDF files. They're hard to read on a phone, and Google can't read what's in them, so people searching 'banana split Scottsdale' may never see your menu."
>
> "So I went ahead and built a free sample of a new site for you. Can I text you the link? It's easiest to see on your phone."

*(Text the link while you're on the phone.)*

> "When it opens, you'll see the full menu right on the page with tabs, a big call button, directions, today's hours with an 'open now' badge, and a Fiesta section so parents can call to book birthday parties. Your family story is there too, with your original 1958 opening ad."

**Ask, then listen:**
- "How do most new customers find you these days? Tourists, locals, Google?"
- "How many birthday parties does the Fiesta room do in a month? Would you want more?"
- "Who takes care of the website now?"

**The offer:**
> "Here's how I work. It's a one-time price of **$[price]**, with no monthly fees to me. You own the site and the domain. I'll update it with your current prices, add your real Google reviews, and have it live in about a week. Your current web address stays the same."

**Close:**
> "Want me to go ahead and set it up?"

- **"I need to talk to my husband / wife / family":** "Of course. It's a family business. I'll text you the link so you can show them. Would Thursday be a good time to follow up?"
- **"How much again?":** Repeat the price. Then: "That's about [price ÷ 10] sundaes' worth of extra customers a month for a year to pay for itself."

---

## Pricing to quote
- **Recommended: $1,800 one-time.** This is an established business, and a quote that's too low makes them doubt the quality.
- **Founding-client price: $1,500** in exchange for permission to use them in your portfolio plus a testimonial. A Sugar Bowl testimonial will impress other local restaurants.
- **Optional upsell: a photo shoot, +$300–$500.** Their current photos are low resolution and from 2018. New photos of sundaes, the soda fountain and the pink booths would make the site, and their Google listing, look much better. (Hire a local food photographer or do it yourself if you can shoot well.)
- **Payment:** 50% to start and 50% at launch.
- **Their only ongoing cost:** the domain they already pay for, plus hosting if they move to a free host.

## Objections
| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "We've been fine for 68 years without a fancy website." | "You absolutely have, and that's why the site should show it. Most first-time visitors and tourists look you up on their phone first. This just makes sure what they see matches the real place." |
| "We get plenty of customers." | "That's great. The site can also help with what you want more of, like Fiesta parties and take-home pints, and it means fewer phone calls asking about hours." |
| "Too expensive." | "It's a one-time cost with no monthly fees, and I can split it into two payments. If it books even two extra birthday parties a month, it pays for itself fast." |
| "Our nephew / a friend handles the website." | "No problem. The sample might give them some ideas, and you're welcome to keep the link." |
| "Can you just put the menu on our current site?" | "I can do that as a smaller job if you prefer. But the speed and phone problems would still be there. The new site fixes all of it at once." |

## After a yes: what to collect
1. A short agreement and a 50% invoice.
2. **Current menu and prices,** plus any changes since January 2025.
3. **Which opening date is right:** New Year's Eve 1958, or the day before Christmas (their 1958 ad says the latter).
4. **Who manages the website and domain now.** Their current site runs on WordPress. You'll need the domain login (or the person who has it) to switch it over.
5. Their favorite Google reviews, and permission to quote them.
6. **Permission for Family Circus artwork.** Only show the cartoons online if they confirm they have the rights. Otherwise, mention it in text only, as the demo does.
7. Fiesta party details: price per party, how many kids, and which days.
