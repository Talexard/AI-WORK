# Pitch Script: Peixoto Coffee

**Call:** the Chandler flagship, **(480) 664-6290**. Gilbert (480) 597-7584 and Downtown Phoenix (480) 597-6038 are backups.
**Online:** their site has a **Wholesale contact form** and a **Jobs** page, and they're active on Instagram as @peixotocoffee. A short email or DM is a good second touch.
**Ask for:** the owner, or whoever handles marketing or the website. It's a family business.
**Demo link:** `[your Netlify link, e.g. peixoto-preview.netlify.app]`
**Price:** $2,400 one-time (3 locations, plus connecting to their existing online store). Testimonial price $2,000. Add-on: monthly content updates (seasonal drinks, new coffees) at $100 per update, or train their staff.

## Best time
- **Call or visit 1–4 pm on a weekday,** after the morning rush.
- **Going in person works well:** buy a coffee at the Chandler flagship and ask the barista who handles the website or marketing. Leave your card and the link with them.

## How this pitch differs
Peixoto is **successful**: three cafés, about 1,400 reviews at 4.7★, an online store and subscriptions. Their site **works**, so don't call it broken. Your angle is that **it hides their best asset.** They grow their own coffee, and almost no roaster can say that. Pitch the new site as a **brand upgrade** that sells more subscriptions and wholesale.

## Call
**Opener (to the barista or manager):**
> "Hi! Quick question: who handles the website or marketing for Peixoto? My name is [Your name], I'm a local web designer, and I put together something I think the owners would love."

- **If it's not them:** "Could I get a name and an email to send it to? Or I can text a link to whoever's best."

**With the owner or marketing person:**
> "I'm a big fan. You're the only roaster in the Valley I know of that grows its own coffee, on your family farm in Brazil. That's an incredible story. But your website still says 2016 at the bottom, and the farm story is tucked away on a back page. Most visitors never see it."
>
> "So I built a free sample of a new homepage. Can I send you the link?"

**While they look:**
> "It opens on your own coffee cherries with 'We grow it. We roast it. We brew it.' Then the journey from the farm to the cup, with your photos of José Augusto and the drying patio. The coffees show tasting notes and link straight into your existing shop, so checkout stays the same. Subscriptions get their own spot, and each café shows whether it's open right now."

**Ask, then listen:**
- "How much of your business is online and subscriptions compared with the cafés?"
- "Is wholesale something you want to grow?"
- "Who built your current site, and are you happy with your online store?"

**Offer and close:**
> "It's a one-time project, $2,400, no monthly fees to me. I'd rebuild the front of the site and keep your store and checkout working underneath, so nothing breaks for subscribers. Live in about two weeks. Want me to put together a proposal?"

## Objections
| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "Our website works fine." | "It does, and I'd keep your store exactly as it is. This is about the first impression: your story and subscriptions front and center instead of a 2016 template." |
| "We just redid our online store." | "Perfect. This sits on top of it. Your products, checkout and subscriptions stay the same." |
| "Too expensive." | "Three cafés plus an online store at a one-time cost. Ten new subscribers paying $23 a month covers it in under a year." |
| "We have an agency." | "No problem. Feel free to share the sample with them as inspiration." |

## After a yes, collect
- Admin access to their WordPress/WooCommerce site, or contact with whoever manages it
- Current photos (cafés, drinks, the roasting) and their brand logo files
- Their Google review count and favorite reviews
- Any seasonal menu they want featured
